## 1. Purpose

This audit checks whether delayed or nonlocal recovery creates a new negative obstruction after the local O1/O2-A response is installed.  It does not attempt to prove repayment exists; total repayment is already fixed by the bridge identity.

The measured obstruction is

\[
[-\mathcal E_{\rm post}(J)]_+/\left(\sqrt{|J|}\log^2p^*\right).
\]

Delayed-risk proxy rows are intervals with either long windows \(h>8192\) or recovery position \(>0.50\).

## 5. Interpretation

If delayed/nonlocal recovery were a separate obstruction, the negative envelope should grow in late recovery bins, long-window bins, or the delayed-risk proxy.  The audit should be read through that lens.

The audit does **not** show such growth.

The global negative maximum remains

\[
0.0394632013,
\]

and it occurs outside the delayed-risk proxy:

\[
\texttt{worst\_delayed\_risk\_proxy}=\texttt{False}.
\]

The delayed-risk proxy itself has smaller maximum:

\[
\texttt{delayed\_risk\_neg\_max}=0.0343723532.
\]

Late recovery is smaller still:

\[
\texttt{late\_recovery\_neg\_max}=0.0187494298.
\]

The correlation between negative size and recovery position is negative:

\[
\operatorname{corr}([-z]_+,\text{recovery position})
=-0.6184559764.
\]

So the bookkeeping direction is favorable: moving later into recovery does not create a larger negative obstruction; it tends to reduce it.

All rows pass the strong empirical threshold:

\[
[-z(J)]_+\le0.05.
\]

Conclusion:

\[
\boxed{
\text{O2-C introduces no new delayed/nonlocal obstruction in the audited range.}
}
\]

The remaining negative side is still the same small LongA centered fluctuation isolated in O2-B, not a separate delayed-recovery failure mode.
